# Top-conference poster quality

A good conference poster is an expert conversation surface: the main contribution is clear at a distance, decisive evidence is easy to inspect, and the method plus any source-grounded boundaries remain scientifically honest.

Aim for the conference character visible across the bundled real posters and PosterSum-like ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR examples: a compact masthead, immediately distinguishable sections, readable evidence, and purposeful information density. This is a visual-review target, never a fixed rail count, template, or deterministic score.

## Content hierarchy

1. Title and authorship establish identity.
2. The central contribution is easy to find without promotional language.
3. Method explains the novel mechanism, architecture, or study design.
4. Primary evidence receives the most visual area and retains conditions, units, baselines, and uncertainty.
5. Any source-grounded limitation or boundary condition bounds the claim; provenance remains legible and compact.

Do not mirror the paper section by section. Omit secondary prose before shrinking type. Use a figure only when it carries evidence; never add decorative scientific-looking graphics. If the PDF yields relevant original figures, use at least one, retain its machine-readable locator, and give it a short interpretive caption and takeaway. A caption explains what to notice; it does not say where the figure came from.

## Layout

- Choose an evidence-driven emphasis. Figure-led, method-led, result-led, balanced, and multi-entry compositions are examples, not a closed set of modes.
- Use a compact conference masthead. A three-column scaffold is familiar for many landscape ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR posters, but the reference, content, and figure geometry may call for another topology. Unequal measures, cross-column figures, sidecars, local subgrids, and deliberate asymmetry are valid.
- Let content determine grouping, order, spans, and local track count. Do not force a seed's visible regions into semantic slots.
- Give display-scale hero text enough inline measure. If a central claim wraps to four or more lines, widen its text block or rebalance the hero before reducing type.
- Align edges and use consistent gutters, but avoid a dashboard of identical cards.
- Make scan sections immediately distinguishable with restrained group-level cues such as a section title treatment, thin rule, local tone, shared boundary, or deliberate inter-group whitespace. Apply the cue to the section as a group; do not repeat a heavy frame around every module.
- Fill the physical page with meaningful regions. Do not stretch a short method block or invent decoration to occupy an empty area.
- Use the page purposefully without forcing a numeric coverage target. Large empty regions, stretched low-information blocks, and cramped content are screenshot-review concerns; wrapper backgrounds never substitute for evidence.
- Use evidence-driven emphasis where it helps the paper. A focal element may be a figure, method stage, equation, result, or concise takeaway, and some posters may use several coordinated entry points instead of one dominant panel.
- Treat physical type measurements as advisory viewing-distance evidence. Resolve weak readability by editing content, measure, hierarchy, or page size; do not encode a universal type-size pass/fail threshold.
- Support fast scanning across title, contribution, method, evidence, any supplied limitations, and sources. A single linear reading path is optional; locally understandable sections and figures may support several useful entry points.

## Typography and color

- Use a restrained type system with strong contrast and viewing-distance body size.
- Derive the typography relationship from the selected reference and user preference. Serif display with sans body, all-sans, and a restrained hybrid are all valid when locally available; never fetch a remote font.
- Keep title, section, body, caption, and provenance roles coherent without turning font choice into a closed style enum. Warn when a requested local font is unavailable rather than silently changing the design intent.
- Keep line lengths readable and use compact labels rather than excessive micro-headings.
- Typeset central source-grounded equations with paper-like fractions, operators, subscripts, and superscripts. Do not expose raw LaTeX syntax or use an equation image when semantic MathML can remain editable.
- Prefer a restrained coordinated palette. One accent family plus neutrals is common, while the reference, user direction, or institutional identity may justify multiple coordinated accents.
- Avoid gradients, glow, playful stickers, and editorial-magazine effects unless the user explicitly requests them.

## Design language

- Keep the title, author, affiliation, and venue identity coherent in a compact masthead. Real conference posters vary substantially in every other figure, table, paragraph, and section relationship.
- Select header treatment, section-heading treatment, module boundary, and inline emphasis independently. A light header does not imply boxed modules; an accent section bar does not imply equal cards.
- Let figure-led organization use open regions and image-led hierarchy, method-led organization use a stronger process spine, and result-led organization use compact comparison emphasis.
- Scan sections are information groups, not mandatory full-width bands or one-to-one columns. Any track count can work when its measures, figures, and reading paths are coherent; indistinguishable full-height boxes with equal chrome and no hierarchy are the failure.
- A figure-rich module should use concise interpretation around readable evidence, not several full-width figures stacked below paper-style paragraphs.
- Preserve aligned edges and reading order across treatments. Diversity must come from the evidence grammar, not random decoration.

## Final gate

Hard gates protect scientific grounding and identity, asset provenance and hashes, offline safety, basic reviewability, and editable delivery. They block unsupported content, broken bindings, unsafe resources, source evidence that does not render, a non-editable export, poster or element-content scroll overflow, overlap between independent poster modules, and visible poster elements outside the physical page. Other geometry and clipping diagnostics, type size, figure ratios, occupancy, density, whitespace, and hierarchy remain advisory measurements for the image-capable review. That review compares the non-authoritative visual reference with the full candidate screenshot; a pass requires no critical issue or directive. DOM measurements do not substitute for visual judgment.
