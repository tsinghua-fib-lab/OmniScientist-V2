# Top-conference poster quality

A good conference poster is an expert conversation surface: the main contribution is clear at a distance, decisive evidence is easy to inspect, and method plus limitations remain scientifically honest.

## Content hierarchy

1. Title and authorship establish identity.
2. One central claim states the contribution without promotional language.
3. Method explains the novel mechanism, architecture, or study design.
4. Primary evidence receives the most visual area and retains conditions, units, baselines, and uncertainty.
5. Limitations bound the claim; provenance remains legible and compact.

Do not mirror the paper section by section. Omit secondary prose before shrinking type. Use a figure only when it carries evidence; never add decorative scientific-looking graphics. If the PDF yields relevant original figures, use at least one and retain its locator, caption, and takeaway.

## Layout

- Select figure-led, method-led, or balanced composition from actual evidence depth.
- Let content determine spans and column count. Unequal columns and deliberate asymmetry are valid.
- Give display-scale hero text enough inline measure. If a central claim wraps to four or more lines, widen its text block or rebalance the hero before reducing type.
- Align edges and use consistent gutters, but avoid a dashboard of identical cards.
- Fill the physical page with meaningful regions. Do not stretch a short method block or invent decoration to occupy an empty area.
- On A0, ordinary body copy must render at 28 CSS px or larger and provenance at 18 CSS px or larger. Semantic regions should cover at least 70% of the page and reach the final 8% of its height without changing the declared physical page dimensions.
- Keep a clear reading path across title, contribution, method, evidence, limitations, and sources.

## Typography and color

- Use a restrained type system with strong contrast and viewing-distance body size.
- Keep line lengths readable and use compact labels rather than excessive micro-headings.
- Use one restrained accent family plus neutrals unless supplied institutional identity requires more.
- Avoid gradients, glow, playful stickers, and editorial-magazine effects unless the user explicitly requests them.

## Final gate

Revise whenever a claim or number lacks a source, decisive evidence is missing, method novelty is unclear, limitations are hidden, a hero claim forms a narrow four-plus-line text block, a figure is illegible, type is too small, content clips or overflows, large scientific regions are mostly empty, or the page feels like a generic dashboard rather than a conference poster.
