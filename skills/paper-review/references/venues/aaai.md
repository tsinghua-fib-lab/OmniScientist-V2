- Use the target year's official main-track criteria and review process. Special tracks require their own official rubric.
- When no year is supplied, use the newest verified guidance and state the assumed year in `Target Venue`.

- Never use the generic fallback form or scores for AAAI.

## Main-Track Review Contract

The officially linked AAAI-26 reviewer instructions recommend the following review structure:

1. `Paper Summary` — approximately 4–10 sentences.
2. `Review Summary` — approximately 4–10 sentences explaining the assessment.
3. `Specific Points Of Feedback` — a concrete bullet list, distinguishing essential revisions from optional improvements.
4. `Overall Rating`
5. `Confidence`

Cover strengths, weaknesses, motivation, novelty, soundness, evidence, related work, reproducibility, limitations, and responsible-research concerns inside the three text fields rather than adding a generic feedback form.

## Overall Rating — AAAI-25 And AAAI-26 Main Technical Track

Use the following `1–10` scale:

- `10` — Top 5% of accepted papers; seminal paper.
- `9` — Top 15% of accepted papers; strong accept.
- `8` — Top 50% of accepted papers; clear accept.
- `7` — Good paper; accept.
- `6` — Marginally above the acceptance threshold.
- `5` — Marginally below the acceptance threshold.
- `4` — Not good enough; reject.
- `3` — Clear reject.
- `2` — Strong reject.
- `1` — Trivial or wrong.

The maximum overall rating is `10`. Confidence uses `1–5`, with `5` as the maximum. These values apply to the main technical track; do not reuse them for AISI or another special track without verifying that track's form.

## Criteria

- significance and novelty;
- theoretical or empirical soundness;
- relevance to the AAAI community;
- clarity of exposition;
- clear problem story and motivation;
- appropriate related-work context;
- baselines, metrics, benchmarks, datasets, and error analysis;
- reproducibility;
- limitations, generalizability, and responsible research.

## Venue-Specific Checks

- Is the problem important to AI and clearly motivated by limitations in the state of the art?
- Is the key novel technical contribution identifiable and appropriately contextualized?
- Is the approach sound, clearly described, and free of unstated critical assumptions?
- Do empirical or theoretical results support the claims?
- Are baselines, metrics, benchmarks, datasets, and error analyses appropriate?
- Is the work described in enough detail to reproduce?
- Are limitations in scope and generalizability explicit?
- Are human-subject, sensitive-data, privacy, bias, or other responsible-research issues handled appropriately?

## Calibration Rules

- Do not apply a rejection prior to Phase 1; assess whether concerns are sufficiently negative to prevent further review.
- Distinguish essential revisions from useful but optional improvements.
- Treat missing assumptions, unsupported claims, or invalid evaluation as decision-relevant only in proportion to their effect on the central contribution.
- Keep the review professional, respectful, and depersonalized; refer to `the paper` or `the work`.
- The AAAI-26 AI-generated supplementary review is a conference pilot and intentionally has no rating or recommendation. Do not confuse that pilot output with this author-facing human-review simulation.
