## Evidence Boundary

- AAAI-27 currently publishes the main-track criteria, two-phase process, AI-assisted-review pilot, responsible-research expectations, and special-track boundaries.
- As of the verification date, AAAI-27 does **not** publish a complete human reviewer form, field labels, confidence legend, or numeric overall-rating legend. Do not present an inherited score scale as official AAAI-27 metadata.
- The officially linked AAAI-26 reviewer document explicitly says reviewers are not required to follow a fixed structure. It recommends a paper summary, review summary, and specific feedback list. The operational contract below normalizes that guidance into stable author-facing fields and appends the human review's overall rating and confidence.
- The exact historical `1–10` rating legend is corroborated by public AAAI-25/AAAI-26 conference-form data, not printed on the public AAAI instruction pages. It is retained for historical simulation and must be labelled accordingly.
- An AAAI AI-generated supplementary review is intentionally non-decisional and contains no rating or recommendation. It is not the same artifact as this author-facing human-review simulation.

## AAAI-27 Current Main-Track Guidance — Form Pending

AAAI-27 officially evaluates and scores main-track submissions on:

- significance and novelty of the problem, method, experiments, or analysis;
- theoretical and/or empirical soundness of the claims;
- relevance to the broad AAAI community;
- clarity of exposition;
- responsible research practice, including human subjects, sensitive data, and data or algorithmic bias;
- reproducibility through proofs, experiment documentation, data, and code as appropriate.

Contributions may be theoretical, methodological, algorithmic, empirical, integrative across subfields, or critical analyses of problematic goals, assumptions, or approaches. Solid work that opens territory, poses valuable questions, or connects areas can be preferred to narrow incremental state-of-the-art gains.

AAAI-27 retains two-phase review:

- Phase 1 uses two human reviews plus one clearly identified AI-generated supplementary review.
- Human ratings and human decision-making determine whether a paper advances; the AI review has no rating or recommendation.
- Papers advancing to Phase 2 receive additional human reviews, followed by author response, reviewer discussion, metareview, and decision.
- AI-generated discussion summaries may assist committee members, but no human reviewer or decision-maker is replaced.

The main submission allows seven pages of main content, with pages eight and nine reserved for references. Supplementary material may be submitted, but reviewers are not required to inspect it; evidence essential to evaluation belongs in the main paper.

## AAAI-26 Historical Review Contract

Use this normalized author-facing structure for AAAI-26, or as an explicitly labelled historical writing fallback when a later form is not public:

1. `Paper Summary`
2. `Review Summary`
3. `Specific Points Of Feedback`
4. `Overall Rating`
5. `Confidence`

These names are an operational normalization of AAAI's recommended review structure, not a claim that the official form mandates five fields with exactly these labels.

## Field Guidance

### Paper Summary

The AAAI-26 instructions recommend approximately 4–10 sentences covering:

- the main contribution in one sentence;
- the core problem;
- the key idea and how it addresses the problem;
- how the idea is realized in an implementation, proof, or study;
- the conclusion claimed from the findings.

Use the evaluator's own accurate understanding, not copied abstract text. Treat the sentence range as guidance rather than a hard author-facing length gate.

### Review Summary

The historical guide recommends approximately 4–10 sentences. Begin with the overall assessment, then summarize clarity, technical and experimental soundness, intellectual novelty, relevance, and the highest-priority improvements. Make the conclusion traceable to evidence rather than repeating the numeric score.

### Specific Points Of Feedback

- Use a concrete bullet list with as much depth as each issue requires.
- Include strengths as well as flaws and shortcomings.
- Separate essential revisions from useful but optional work.
- Pair each concern with a realistic suggestion for improvement.
- A related-work request should name relevant sources and explain their relation. The AAAI-26 guide says such a request needs at least three citations from non-overlapping author groups to count as substantive.
- Keep typographical comments short and do not let them dominate technical feedback.

### Overall Rating

For AAAI-26, use the historical main-track `1–10` scale below. For AAAI-27, write `Pending official AAAI-27 form` and provide a qualitative evidence-based conclusion unless the output is explicitly requested as an AAAI-26-scale simulation.

### Confidence

For AAAI-26 historical simulation, use `1–5`, with larger values indicating greater relevant expertise and more careful checking. Confidence measures the evaluator, not the paper. Exact anchor prose is not printed in the public AAAI pages; do not invent a quotation.

## Historical Overall Rating — AAAI-25 And AAAI-26 Main Track

Use only for the stated historical years or an explicitly labelled historical-scale simulation:

- `10` — top 5% of accepted papers; seminal paper;
- `9` — top 15% of accepted papers; strong accept;
- `8` — top 50% of accepted papers; clear accept;
- `7` — good paper; accept;
- `6` — marginally above the acceptance threshold;
- `5` — marginally below the acceptance threshold;
- `4` — not good enough; reject;
- `3` — clear reject;
- `2` — strong reject;
- `1` — trivial or wrong.

The maximum is `10`; historical confidence is `1–5`. Do not reuse these numbers for AAAI-27 special tracks or describe them as a published AAAI-27 legend.

## Technical Review Checks

### Problem And Contribution

- Is the problem important to AI and motivated by a real limitation, gap, or opportunity?
- Is the principal theoretical, methodological, algorithmic, empirical, integrative, or critical contribution identifiable?
- Does the paper explain why the contribution matters beyond a narrow benchmark delta?
- Are novelty claims accurately scoped relative to related and concurrent work?

### Soundness And Evidence

- Are the approach, assumptions, definitions, derivations, and algorithms correct and described clearly?
- Do theoretical arguments prove the stated claims under visible and reasonable assumptions?
- Do experiments actually test the hypotheses and support the conclusions?
- Are baselines, metrics, benchmarks, datasets, splits, statistical methods, and error analyses appropriate?
- Are alternative explanations, failure modes, and uncertainty considered?

### Reproducibility And Limits

- Is enough information supplied to reproduce the core result or independently scrutinize it?
- Are implementation details, proofs, preprocessing, hyperparameters, compute, data access, and evaluation protocols documented in proportion to the claims?
- Are scope, generalizability, resource requirements, and failure cases stated honestly?
- Is essential evidence present in the main paper rather than hidden in optional supplementary material?

### Responsible Research

- Are human-subject approvals, consent, participant treatment, and compensation appropriate?
- Are sensitive data, privacy, security, copyright, licenses, bias, fairness, and foreseeable misuse handled responsibly?
- Does the work overgeneralize beyond represented populations, tasks, domains, or languages?
- Are suspected integrity or policy issues described neutrally and escalated through the appropriate process rather than declared as settled misconduct?

## Track Boundaries — AAAI-27

- `Main Technical Track`: uses the common criteria above.
- `AI For Social Impact`: uses a different rubric emphasizing the importance of the social problem, engagement with domain literature, fit and justification of the AI approach, evaluation quality, follow-up enablement, and credible social impact rather than technical novelty alone.
- `AI Alignment`: emphasizes technical correctness, appropriate related-work coverage, and relevance to alignment topics; its schedule and detailed instructions are separate.

Do not average or translate ratings between these tracks. If the target track is not explicit, ask for or state a main-track assumption.

## Review Style And Process Rules

- Keep the review professional and depersonalized; use `the paper`, `the work`, or `the project` rather than `you`, `the authors`, or remarks about individuals.
- Ask how the paper could be improved, not merely what is wrong with it.
- Make revision requests proportionate to the available time and resources.
- Distinguish essential repairs from nice-to-have extensions.
- In Phase 1, focus on whether the human evidence supports advancement rather than applying an arbitrary rejection quota.
- During author response and discussion, update findings or ratings when a misunderstanding is resolved and explain why.

## Calibration Rules

- Do not reward a narrow state-of-the-art increase automatically, and do not penalize a solid new direction merely for lacking such an increase.
- Separate novelty, soundness, significance, relevance, clarity, reproducibility, and reviewer confidence.
- Treat unsupported central claims, invalid evaluation, or hidden critical assumptions in proportion to their effect on the contribution.
- Do not let an AI-generated supplementary review override direct paper evidence or human judgment.
- Label every use of a historical form or numeric scale when reviewing for a year whose form is not yet public.
- Keep all findings evidence-grounded, constructive, and actionable.
